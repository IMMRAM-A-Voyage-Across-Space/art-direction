# IMMRAM — Commander Vignettes

> Short character pieces for the 18 commanders (3 per isle). Companion to
> `docs/lore/world-backstories.md` and the mechanical `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-06-27-factions-design.md`.
> Each commander's kit (passive · active · lean) is written *into* the character, not listed as stats.
> At run start you pick one; the other two of that isle rally to the opposing cause and hunt you.

---

## 01 · Mag Mell

### Bríd, the Kindler — *Tuatha Bláth · economy & defense*
> "We didn't break this plain to hand it back. Dig in."

Bríd came up planting windbreaks and grain, not fighting wars — a third-generation sower who reads
soil better than a chart. She leads Tuatha Bláth the way she farmed: patiently, cheaply, holding
ground others would abandon. Her crews love her because she spends ammunition like water in a
drought and eats last. When the Directorate's columns come, Bríd doesn't charge — she **entrenches**,
roots her stacks into the fields they grew, and lets the attacker break on a line that simply will
not move.

### Medb, the Ledger-Queen — *An Ard-Riail · logistics & firepower*
> "Sentiment is a supply problem. I have solved it."

Medb never set foot on a machaire field. She runs An Ard-Riail the way one runs a fleet — by
manifest, by margin, by the cold arithmetic of who can be resupplied fastest. To her, Tuatha Bláth
aren't traitors so much as an inefficiency: an isle's worth of production sitting outside the
admiralty's ledger. She wins by **out-growing** her enemy, fielding fresh stacks faster than they
fall, and when a position proves stubborn she removes it from orbit. She is not cruel. She is simply
never surprised.

### Fergus, the Ironside — *hardliner · frontline combat*
> "It held because I was on it."

Fergus is the soldier both causes wish they'd kept. A career officer who's served three admiralties
and outlived all of them, he believes in the line, the man beside him, and very little else — least
of all whichever flag is paying this season. Where Medb commands from a screen, Fergus stands in the
front rank taking the **retaliation** himself, and his stacks hit back twice as hard for the shame
of letting him. Give him a wall to hold and he is immovable; give him a reason and he'll **rally**
the whole line over it.

## 02 · Tír na nÓg

### Dian Cécht — *Na Draoithe · sustain & biomass*
> "Nothing is lost here. It is only re-sung."

Dian Cécht is not entirely a person anymore. Once a wandering healer, they let the Druids' spores
root in their nervous system to *translate* for it, and now speak in a chorus of voices, none of
them only their own. Dian Cécht fights the way a forest heals a burn: slowly, inevitably,
**regenerating** every wound the enemy opens until attrition runs the wrong way. They do not hate
the Grafted. They grieve them — the way you grieve a limb that has decided to become someone else.

### Miach, the Grafter — *Na Beangáin · mutation & aggression*
> "Why inherit a body when you can author one?"

Miach has replaced so much of himself that no two sources agree on what he started as. Each kill is,
to him, *material* — a chance to graft something stronger onto the host and onto his own
ever-shifting frame, so he grows **stronger with every stack he feeds to the spores**. He finds the
Druids' harmony obscene: a whole isle of perfect biology, content merely to *survive*. Miach does
not survive. He improves, violently.

### Naomh Folamh, the Hollow Saint — *zealot · swarm & control*
> "Be still. The bloom is coming for you, and it is patient."

No one knows which cause Naomh Folamh began in, only that the Druids' song hollowed them out and
filled them back up with something quieter and more certain. They move at the head of a tide of
sporelings, and enemies near them find their limbs slow and their orders forgotten — **the air
itself seems to drink intent**. The Saint rarely strikes. The Saint simply arrives, and waits, and
lets the swarm wake.

## 03 · Gorias

### Balor of the Evil Eye — *Na Fomóiri · economy & attrition*
> "Everything that dies on my sand pays rent."

Balor built her clan out of three wrecked hulls and a stubborn refusal to die, and she's run it like
a salvage yard ever since: nothing wasted, everyone useful, every casualty stripped for parts before
it's cold. She fights to *profit* — her war chest **swells off the battlefield's dead**, hers and
theirs alike — and her mechs walk away from fights that should have scrapped them, because Balor is
already out there welding them back together. Loyalty on Gorias is cheap. Balor pays better.

### Bres, the Blighted — *An Creimeadh · corrosion & terror*
> "All things return to rust. I merely keep the schedule."

Bres speaks softly, through a filter mask, in the unhurried tone of someone who has already seen how
everything ends. An Creimeadh's doctrine is his theology: armor, walls, pride, the body itself —
all of it temporary, and the acid simply tells the truth faster. His attacks **eat the enemy's
defenses away** layer by layer until there is nothing left to hide behind, and then it rains. He is
not angry. He is, if anything, gentle. That is the worst part.

### Fáidh na Meirge, the Rust Prophet — *warlord · swarm & zealotry*
> "The desert doesn't count its dead. Neither do I. *Surge.*"

Fáidh na Meirge preaches from a pulpit of welded scrap to a congregation that mostly explodes. He
promises the faithful a simple paradise — more of them, always more — and delivers, **conjuring
fresh waves** out of the wreckage faster than they can be put down. Where Balor values every part and
Bres values the lesson, the Prophet values only the tide. He has never feared being outnumbered. He
has only ever feared being still.

## 04 · Murias

### Cathair, the Warden — *Dún Seaca · fortification & defense*
> "We do not take ground. We refuse to give it."

Cathair has held the same line so long the ice has grown over the old battlements in the shape of
his stubbornness. He is a man of walls — built, defended, believed in — and under his command a
stack that **plants its feet** becomes nearly impossible to move, while he raises fresh ramparts of
ice between his people and the cold beyond. Dún Seaca's enemies have learned the lesson the first
winter taught: you do not break Cathair. You only freeze waiting for him to break first.

### Scáthach, the Vanguard — *An Díorma Oighir · ambush & control*
> "The cold is already inside your line. I just told it where to bite."

Scáthach learned to hunt in white-out, where the difference between predator and corpse is who
moves first and is seen last. She has no patience for Cathair's walls — a wall only tells the enemy
where you are. Her freeze doesn't just chill; it **steals**, locking an enemy's turn out from under
them so her raiders strike into a line that cannot strike back. By the time the storm clears she's
gone, and so is whatever she came for.

### An Doleáite, the Thawless — *zealot · endurance & control*
> "Cold is a question. We stopped answering."

An Doleáite walks Murias's killing winters bare-faced, and the cold no longer seems to notice
her — nor do the freeze-weapons of any enemy who tries to **slow** her. She leads the few who have
stopped fearing the dark entirely, and where she stands the world locks: a whole field of ice
sealing shut, denying movement to everyone but An Doleáite and her own. She is not warm. She has
simply made peace with never being warm again.

## 05 · Tech Duinn

### Goibniu, the Forgemaster — *Na Gaibhne · armor & industry*
> "Fire does not destroy iron. It teaches it shape."

Goibniu's hands are scarred to the elbow and he counts the burns as scripture. To Na Gaibhne, fire
is the first and best craftsman, and Goibniu is its high priest — **armoring** his juggernauts in
the same heat that would kill lesser hosts and **reforging** the broken ones mid-battle as calmly as
a smith straightening a blade. He is slow, deliberate, and nearly unbreakable, and he regards the
Cinderborn the way any maker regards a child playing with matches: with grief, and a little fear.

### Aodh, the Ashlord — *Clann na Luaithe · burn & aggression*
> "I am the spark that doesn't care what it lands on."

Aodh burns. Everything he touches catches; everywhere he fights, the ground is still smoldering
when he's gone. He has no use for Goibniu's patience — fire isn't a tool to him, it's the truth,
and the only honest thing to do with truth is spread it. His hosts leave the enemy **alight and
dwindling**, and Aodh fights hottest once the battle is already burning out of control — which, to
him, is simply when it gets honest.

### Naomh na Luaithe, the Cinder Saint — *fire-cult · sacrifice & frenzy*
> "Give the fire what it asks. It only ever asks for everything."

Naomh na Luaithe leads from within the blaze and asks of their followers the one thing every faith
eventually asks: everything. They will **spend half a stack to make the other half burn twice as
bright**, and the faithful go gladly, because the Saint goes first and goes furthest. Wounded, they
grow *more* dangerous — a candle guttering hottest at the end. Even the Cinderborn, who fear little,
lower their voices when the Saint walks past.

## 06 · Falias

### Sreng, the Foreman — *Cumann na Piocóide · economy & swarm*
> "One drill breaks. Ten thousand don't stop. That's the whole speech."

Sreng came up in the deep cuts and never learned to talk like management, which is exactly why the
Union follows him. He fights the way Falias mines: with patient, overwhelming **mass** — more
drones, more bodies, more output than any precise little order can grind down. Call him stubborn and
he'll agree; call him outnumbered and he'll laugh, because there is always another shift coming up
the lift. Sreng doesn't win battles so much as refuse, at industrial scale, to lose them.

### Tost, the Silent — *An tOrd Tostach · stealth & precision*
> *[no transmission]*

Tost took the vow of the vacuum: no voice, no signature, no warning. Her order is few and devout
and almost never seen — which is the point. Her stacks open every battle already **gone from the
enemy's sensors**, and when she **marks** a target the whole Order's fire finds it at once, out of
silence. Sreng fields a crowd; Tost fields a rumor that turns out, too late, to have been standing
behind you. She and the Hermit are the only two on Falias who understand each other, and they have
never once spoken.

### Díthreabhach an Fholúis, the Vacuum Hermit — *savant · mobility & recon*
> "Gravity is a suggestion. Distance is a habit. I have quit both."

The Hermit lives alone on the far craters with their drones for company and a map of Falias more
complete than the Union's surveys and the Order's intelligence combined. They fight rarely and
strangely — **flyers and drones moving farther and seeing more than physics should allow**, a lone
stack leaping craters in the low gravity to land where no one was watching. Both causes court the
Hermit. The Hermit watches both, helps whoever is more interesting that week, and tells neither the
truth.
