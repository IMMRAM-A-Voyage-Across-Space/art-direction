# IMMRAM — World & Faction Backstories

> Narrative lore for the six mythic isles and their twelve causes. Companion to the mechanical
> `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-06-27-factions-design.md`. Tone and format follow the established
> **Mag Mell** backstory in `art/commission2.html` (§3.0).
>
> IMMRAM is an **immram** — a voyage-tale in the old Irish way: a clan-host sailing the star-sea
> from isle to mythic isle, each one stranger than the last. The through-line of every world: a
> single biome breeds **one ecology** — one shared roster — and **two causes** lay claim to it under
> opposing banners. The army you command is the army you meet across the table: *the same crew,
> rowing for a different chief.* Every war here is, at heart, a family argument fought with the
> same hands.

## 01 · Mag Mell — Machaire *(green plain / water)*
*Tuatha Bláth · An Ard-Riail ("The Directorate")*

**Commanders:** Bríd, the Kindler *(Tuatha Bláth)* · Medb, the Ledger-Queen *(An Ard-Riail)* · Fergus, the Ironside *(hardliner)*

*(Canonical Mag Mell backstory — also carried in `art/commission2.html` §3.0; keep the two in sync.)*

Mag Mell was the clan-host's first landfall beyond the home star — the Plain of Delight, a green
world the early voyage-fleets broke and sowed into the **breadbasket of the star-sea**. For a
generation it was the jewel: forest-belt holds, endless machaire fields, the isle every later
landfall measured itself against. "Mag Mell" became the word for *the good years* — the name the
crews gave to a season when the voyage still felt like a promise instead of a fight.

Then the home fleet reasserted itself. **An Ard-Riail** — the centralised admiralty and its
professional, plasma-armed hosting — moved to bring the isle, its resources, and its sea-lanes back
under direct command; **Medb, the Ledger-Queen** speaks for the order it promises, **Fergus, the
Ironside** for the iron behind it. **Tuatha Bláth** — the folk of blossom, the settler-line who
broke the plain with their own hands — refused, holding that Mag Mell belongs to those who made it
bloom, and **Bríd, the Kindler**, would sooner entrench in the fields than hand them to a flag.

So IMMRAM's first war is a *civil* one: the same folk, the same warriors, the same craft, split
over who Mag Mell belongs to and how the clan-host should sail on. Óglach, Ceithearnach, Gallóglach,
Fomhórach — every host you raise has a mirror across the table rowing under the other colour. That
is why it is the isle you start on: familiar, warm, and quietly heartbreaking.

## 02 · Tír na nÓg — Draíocht *(bioluminescent growth / algal water)*
*Na Draoithe · Na Beangáin ("The Grafted")*

**Commanders:** Dian Cécht *(Na Draoithe)* · Miach, the Grafter *(Na Beangáin)* · Naomh Folamh, the Hollow Saint *(zealot)*

Tír na nÓg is not so much an isle as a single breathing thing. Beneath every glowing fern and
tide-pool runs one luminous mycelial web, and the web **remembers** — the ground itself thinks,
slowly, in spore-song and chemical tides. The clan-host's survey-craft came for the miracle
biology: tissue that heals anything, blooms that cure anything. They never fully left, and some of
them never stayed quite themselves.

**Na Draoithe** — the Druids — are the isle waking to defend itself: a gestalt hive-mind with no
single voice, only harmony, regrowing every wound faster than a blade can open it. Against them
stand **Na Beangáin**: the wandering healers and their descendants who stopped studying Tír na nÓg
and started *wearing* it, splicing vine to flesh and spore to bone until they became mutating,
deathless things that see the Druids not as kin but as raw material. **Dian Cécht** sings to keep
the chorus whole; **Miach, the Grafter** grows stronger from everything that dies near him.

So every beast you field — sporeling, vine, behemoth — is grown from the same biomass. The only
question is whether that life is a song to be kept in tune, or clay to be remade. To fight on Tír
na nÓg is to decide what *alive* should even mean.

## 03 · Gorias — Meirg *(rust-red iron dunes / acid)*
*Na Fomóiri · An Creimeadh ("the corrosion")*

**Commanders:** Balor of the Evil Eye *(Na Fomóiri)* · Bres, the Blighted *(An Creimeadh)* · Fáidh na Meirge, the Rust Prophet *(warlord)*

Gorias is a graveyard that learned to walk. An earlier, prouder wave of the voyage made landfall
here, choked on the acid air, and died in their thousands — leaving a rust-red desert buried under
the bones of drowned hulls. The clan that survives now are the children of that failure, and they
live the only way the dunes allow: by eating the dead. Every horizon is wreckage; every breath is
filtered; every craft is stitched from three older ones.

**Na Fomóiri** — Fomorian raiders — are the scrapper clans who hold that survival *is* rebuilding:
a wreck is a promise, a salvaged bolt is sacred, and the future is welded together one ruin at a
time. **An Creimeadh** learned a darker lesson from the same poison: that the acid which killed the
first landing is not a curse but a doctrine. They melt armor and certainty alike, preach that
nothing deserves to last, and call the rust holy. **Balor of the Evil Eye** counts every rivet as
treasure; **Bres, the Blighted** would dissolve the isle down and feel cleaner for it.

Both hosts ride the same jury-rigged acid-rigs and salvage-walkers. One side patches them like
family heirlooms; the other lets them rot into something crueler on purpose. Gorias asks whether
you build from the ruin — or finish what the rust started.

## 04 · Murias — Oighear *(methane ice / liquid methane)*
*Dún Seaca ("Frostbound Hold") · An Díorma Oighir ("Glacial Vanguard")*

**Commanders:** Cathair, the Warden *(Dún Seaca)* · Scáthach, the Vanguard *(An Díorma Oighir)* · An Doleáite, the Thawless *(zealot)*

Murias should have killed its landing in the first winter. The cold here is not weather; it is the
law, the enemy, and the landlord — a methane-ice isle where the dark lasts months and the air
freezes the unwary mid-breath. The clan that remains are the descendants of those who *didn't* die:
who dug into the ice, walled themselves behind it, and — slowly, grudgingly — learned to make the
freeze take orders.

**Dún Seaca** believes survival is endurance: hold the wall, yield nothing, and let the cold break
the attacker the way it broke everything before them. **An Díorma Oighir** believes the cold is a
blade, not a shield — they weaponise the white-out, freeze an enemy in the middle of a step, and are
gone before the storm clears. Same freeze-craft, opposite faith: **Cathair, the Warden** raises ice
ramparts and waits behind them; **Scáthach, the Vanguard** turns the blizzard into a knife and
never waits at all.

To fight on Murias is to choose between the wall and the ambush — between outlasting the cold and
*becoming* it. The colossus that shields one host's line is the same beast that freezes the other's
prey.

## 05 · Tech Duinn — Gríosach *(black volcanic rock / lava)*
*Na Gaibhne ("Emberwrights") · Clann na Luaithe ("Cinderborn")*

**Commanders:** Goibniu, the Forgemaster *(Na Gaibhne)* · Aodh, the Ashlord *(Clann na Luaithe)* · Naomh na Luaithe, the Cinder Saint *(fire-cult)*

Tech Duinn is the isle of the dead — the far shore every immram fears, where the fire is the only
thing that ever loved anyone. Warmth, light, water, metal — all of it comes up out of the lava, so
the lava became god. Holds cling to the magma channels like moths; the great forges have not gone
cold in living memory; every child is born within sight of a fire that could kill them and keeps
them alive instead. To worship the burn here is not superstition. It is just paying attention.

**Na Gaibhne** are forge-priests who venerate fire as a *maker* — patient, armored, hammering
molten war-juggernauts into being and treating heat as a discipline. **Clann na Luaithe** are the
heretics who venerate fire as a *destroyer* — a frenzied warband that burns brightest closest to
death, spends its own flesh for one more blaze, and holds that anything which can be consumed
deserves to be. **Goibniu, the Forgemaster** reforges what shatters and armors what survives;
**Naomh na Luaithe, the Cinder Saint** feeds the pyre with their own stacks and calls it grace.

The same magma-drakes and molten titans march under both banners. One side tempers them into
anvils; the other throws them onto the fire. Tech Duinn asks the oldest question its fire ever
posed — and the last one, since it is the final isle of the voyage: does the flame exist to forge,
or to consume?

## 06 · Falias — Cnámh *(bone-grey stone dust & craters / the Veil — cold-trap mist)*
*Cumann na Piocóide ("Pickaxe Union") · An tOrd Tostach ("the silent order")*

**Commanders:** Sreng, the Foreman *(Cumann na Piocóide)* · Tost, the Silent *(An tOrd Tostach)* · Díthreabhach an Fholúis, the Vacuum Hermit *(savant)*

*Design note: the Veil is mechanically just Falias's liquid, and a guide is its ferry — any
expedition can pay for one, so it gates nothing. It changes no game systems; only the theme and
visuals differ.*

Falias is bone-grey, airless, and rich — a low-gravity moon-isle cratered to the core and threaded
with mines, the resource heart that the rest of the fleet quietly depends on. But the vacuum hides
a trap: in the deep, sunless crater-floors, cold-trapped heavy gases settle into drifting banks of
grey mist — **the Veil** — opaque, disorienting, and quietly lethal to anyone who wanders in without
knowing the way back out. Wealth and silence in equal measure, over a fog that swallows the
careless whole.

**Cumann na Piocóide** is the miners' labor-militia — tens of thousands of hardhats and drilling
drones who hold that the isle belongs to the hands that dig it, and who win the only way labor ever
does: together, in numbers, out-massing anyone who'd take the lode. **An tOrd Tostach** is a
vacuum-monastic sect sworn to the quiet and the dark — and, crucially, *they are the ones who know
the Veil.* To cross the crater-mist alive you need a **guide**, and the Order's veiled adepts have
charted its safe paths in silence for generations (the **Vacuum Hermit** knows them better than
anyone alive). Where **Sreng, the Foreman** floods the open dust with bodies and machines, **Tost,
the Silent's** few move whole hosts through fog no one else dares enter.

The loud many against the silent few, over the same tunnels, rails, and mass-drivers — and the Veil
between them. The Union owns the open ground; the Order owns the grey. To campaign on Falias is to
learn which paths through the mist are real, and who is willing to lead you down them.

## Notes & hooks for later

- **Commanders:** each isle has a third commander (a zealot/savant — Naomh Folamh, Fáidh na Meirge,
  An Doleáite, Naomh na Luaithe, Díthreabhach an Fholúis, Fergus the Ironside) ripe for short
  character vignettes.
- **Neutrals:** the apex wildlife (Grazer-Titan, Pollen Wraith, Dune Serpent, Hoarfrost Drake,
  Magma Leviathan, Void Stalker) and the universal mercenaries each want a line or two of myth.
- **Throughline:** every war is the same crew fighting for opposite reasons — worth a one-page
  framing essay tying the six civil arguments into IMMRAM's larger immram spine: how a clan-host
  should sail the star-sea, and what's worth landing for.
