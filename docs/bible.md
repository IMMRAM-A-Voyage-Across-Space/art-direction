## The through-line

**Every war here is a family argument fought with the same hands.**

**IMMRAM** is an **immram** — a voyage-tale in the old Irish way. Its cast are the **grandchildren of
the immrama**: descendants of the legendary voyagers (Máel Dúin, Bran, Brendan) who inherit the old
sea-roads and sail them out among the stars. A single biome breeds **one ecology**: one shared
six-tier roster. **Two causes** lay claim to it under opposing banners, so the army you command is
the army you meet across the table — *the same crew, rowing for a different chief.* This document is
the art brief for that world: its look, its naming, and every drawing an artist needs — with a
checklist per isle.

Planet · Biome
:   Each isle has a mythic planet-name and a biome-name. Art is authored per biome; the fort/citadel
    is themed per world.

Naming
:   Irish first, English gloss after. Unit tiers run T1 (chaff) → T6 (champion) across three
    classes: melee, ranged, flyer.

Deliverables
:   Every isle has a checklist of exactly what to draw — terrain, structures, units, commanders, and
    its apex beast.

## The Voyage of the Grandchild

*The story we're sailing.*

A story **tutorial-campaign** sits atop the game; the game itself stays a **roguelike-lite** —
procedural voyages, permadeath. The campaign follows **Máel Dúin's grandchild**, who inherits an
unfinished wound and a fixed destination: the ones responsible. Across a handful of isles the
grandchild is changed, until — like the ancestor — they can **choose mercy instead of the kill**.

**The turn is the whole design.** The campaign is a voyage with one fixed destination — vengeance.
Completing it, by choosing forgiveness, is what **releases** the player into the open sea: the
endless roguelike, with no destination at all. Letting go of the one place you were sailing toward
is what opens the whole ocean.

!!! warning "The one rule — echoes, not transcription"

    We inherit the mood and shape of the old tales; we never re-stage them literally.

    **Do**

    - Inherit the **mood and shape** of an ancestral wonder — invent the matter in space
    - “Ants that eat the boat” → a scavenger-swarm stripping a derelict
    - “Land of eternal youth” → a planet that tempts you to stop voyaging
    - Cloud · Tree · Star recur as half-remembered legend-motifs, not stage props

    **Don't**

    - Re-stage the tales literally (no literal Isle of Ants)
    - Treat any island list as a locked 1:1 map
    - Lose the grandchildren's distance — they half-remember these legends
    - Forget the mood arc: grief/hardness early, wonder/release as it opens out

**Two bloods per isle — a visual tell.** One cause is led by **grandchildren of the immrama** (the
rooted side that tends the isle) — draw them **carrying their lineage**: knotwork bloodline-marks, an
ancestral crest worn like a badge. The rival cause are the **“new”** (the side that consumes or
remakes it) — draw their heraldry **bare of the old marks, or effacing them**. Whether they were ever
kin is a mystery the art should *hint at, never answer.*

## Neon Celtic Pop

*The look.*

Three things at once. Retro-futurist neon, Insular-Celtic ornament, and pop-poster punch — chunky
pixel art where a vast dark ground makes a few saturated neons sing, and gold Celtic knotwork lays
the *ancient* over the *futuristic*. Think a Book-of-Kells page rebuilt as a neon arcade cabinet.

### Neon on the dark

Space is vast and near-black. Grounds stay dark and desaturated so a small number of glowing neons
carry all the energy. Bloom lives on the neons only — never the grounds.

### Celtic gold

Insular-Irish ornament in gold — interlace, spirals, key-frets — trims chrome, structures, and
emblems. It reads as sacred craft laid over the tech, and blooms softly like the other lights.

### Pop punch

Bold, high-contrast, readable. Chunky silhouettes you recognise at a glance; clean shapes; nothing
muddy or fussy. If it doesn't read as a poster at thumbnail size, simplify.

### What it looks like

- Dark ground · 1–2 neon accents · bloom on the light
- Gold Celtic ornament over a tech panel
- Bold silhouette · reads at thumbnail size

### Colour — guidance, not a lock

Each world has its own mood (the green plain, the rust dunes, the ice, the lava, the bone-grey
moon). Pick what serves the scene — these are the only hard rules:

- Grounds near-black & desaturated
- 1–2 saturated neons per scene
- Gold = all ornament
- Bloom on neon + gold only
- Player = cyan rim · Enemy = magenta rim

The exact ground, ornament-gold and rim values are locked on the [Palette](palette.md) page; the
per-isle accents below are the mood, not the whole set.

!!! tip "Lean in"

    - Silhouette first, detail second
    - Consistent top-left key light across everything
    - Let big dark areas breathe
    - Save saturation for what matters

!!! danger "Avoid"

    - Rainbow scenes — too many competing neons
    - Bloom/haze on terrain and mid-tones
    - Filigree so fine it won't survive the pixel grid
    - Muddy mid-value soup with no black anchor

### Reference wells — the other two thirds

The source tales feed the *myth*; these feed the *look*. Read for calibration, then bend to our
palette and pixel grid.

**Space — vast & empty**

- [1970s SF paperback art](https://www.counter-print.co.uk/products/worlds-beyond-time-sci-fi-art-of-the-1970s)
  — Chris Foss, **John Harris** (monumental structures in hazy light), Peter Elson
- [Moebius / *Arzach*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Giraud) — the lone-voyage-through-wonder
  mood (clean horizons; not his gritty cyberpunk)

**Neon pop — retro-futurist skin**

- [Signalnoise “Outrun”](https://signalnoise.com/outrun-series) — magenta/cyan/violet neon; our
  cyan/magenta rim logic
- [Hyper Light Drifter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyper_Light_Drifter) — the north star: pixel
  art, neon-on-dark. If you look at one, this

No reference has our actual fusion — illuminated-manuscript **gold knotwork over synthwave neon**.
These wells feed the halves; the cross is ours.

## Celtic Filigree

*The ornament layer.*

The gold vocabulary — the curls, twists, and knots that mark this world as Celtic. Use it on UI
chrome, structure trim, faction emblems, and borders; keep it off unit silhouettes so they still
read. These are motif references, not final pixel art — the drawing job is to translate them into
the chunky gold-on-dark look above.

| Motif | Reads as |
|---|---|
| Spiral (curl) | the single boss |
| Triskele | triple spiral |
| Double spiral | S-scroll |
| Key fret | step / meander border |
| Plait (twist) | two-strand weave |
| Knot | interlace ring |
